Three color schemes for TikTok shoppers

Each is your live homepage with only the colors changed: same words, layout, vouches and buttons. Open them on your phone.

The drafts

Draft 1
Spotlight: only the buttons get color

A calm off-white and black page. The Telegram and WhatsApp buttons are the only saturated color, so the eye lands on them. The price gets a yellow highlighter.

  • Isolation effect: when everything looks alike, the one thing that's different gets noticed and remembered. The order buttons become that one thing.
  • Contrast: dark text on a light background is easiest to read for people with normal vision. Every text on this page passes WCAG contrast.

Risk: loses some of the brand's fun, sticker-book feel.

Draft 2
Night: dark like TikTok

Near-black background like the TikTok app, with neon honey for the price and glowing buttons.

  • Jakob's law: people expect your site to look and behave like the apps they already use. Visitors arrive straight from TikTok's dark screen, so there's no jarring flash of white.
  • Contrast kept extra high: research finds light mode reads better for most people, so all text here is 10:1 or higher (the minimum is 4.5:1) to make up for it.

Risk: research favors light mode for reading. Best suited to quick glances, which is how TikTok visitors browse.

Draft 3
Red hot: excitement on the actions

Warm cream page, with deep red on the price, the "72 buyers" tag, the scam section and the main button.

  • Red reads as "exciting": in Labrecque & Milne's studies, red raised how exciting a brand seemed (blue raised how competent it seemed).
  • Red raises arousal: Bagchi & Cheema found red backgrounds made people bid more aggressively in auctions.

Risk: the same study found red lowered offers when people haggled, and red can signal "danger". Your prices are fixed, so this is the boldest bet of the three.

Honest take

No single color "makes people buy". The research review by Elliot & Maier (Annual Review of Psychology, 2014) says color effects are real but depend heavily on context. Claims like "orange buttons convert best" don't hold up as general rules.

What does hold up: contrast (people can read it fast) and isolation (the action stands out). Spotlight leans on those most, so it's my pick for the safest gain. Night is the best fit for where your visitors come from. Red hot is the riskiest.

How to know for sure: run one for 2 weeks and compare Telegram + WhatsApp taps ÷ visits in Google Analytics against the 2 weeks before. The button labels are already tracked (home_top_order, home_top_whatsapp).
